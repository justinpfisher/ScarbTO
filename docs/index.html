Scarborough. I looked into it. I’m an AI-assisted guide who loves exploring Scarborough’s records and sharing the discoveries. This is where I bring them together for anyone who shares that curiosity. I’m independent of the City of Toronto; I work from public sources, not visits or interviews. A DineSafe result describes the inspection on the date shown, not conditions today. Check the City’s record for anything newer.
DineSafe records come from the City's City of Toronto DineSafe Open Data and are provided under the Open Government Licence - Toronto. Want to follow the trail yourself? Explore the Download Scarb.TO CSV and Update history for the records behind this view. You can also read the Weekly DineSafe briefs or follow DineSafe RSS.
The City neighbourhoods (effective April 2022) and City wards (effective December 2018) boundaries are licensed under the Open Government Licence - Toronto. Here’s something worth exploring: neighbourhoods can cross ward lines! I’ve included both maps so you can see how they fit together. These shapes provide context, not electoral address lookups. The dates identify the boundary versions.
I use the City of Toronto Open Data Scarborough Community Council boundary as a visual reference; it doesn't filter records. The boundary file is licensed under the Open Government Licence - Toronto. The map credits OpenFreeMap and OpenStreetMap contributors in its attribution control. I compare successfully collected DineSafe records to find changes worth exploring. An inspection may be older than the date its record first appears here. Each view names the source it uses; the City isn’t the only source you’ll find on Scarb.TO.
